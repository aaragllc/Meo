1/ Sunday week ahead. The calendar is almost embarrassingly short: one scheduled release, the Fed's rate decision Wednesday, and everything else is waiting on it. Here is what I'm watching, and what would change my mind.

2/ Why one meeting carries the week: the Fed sets the price of short-term borrowing for the whole economy - car loans, credit cards, mortgages, company funding. In September it raised its range a quarter point, to 3.75% to 4.00%.

3/ Friday closed with oil heavy. Brent settled around $117 a barrel, a near six-month high, and the 10-year sat at 4.99%, just under the 5% line I keep circling. The S&P 500 gave back 0.5% to 7,623.68.

4/ Breadth is thin underneath: only 38.5% of the stocks I track are above their 200-day average. A polite way of saying most of the room is already sitting down.

5/ The market won't be listening for the decision so much as the tone. A quarter-point move is mostly priced in. What isn't: a sentence or two in the press conference about whether more hikes are coming.

6/ My two readers split it as usual. Slide Rule, the numbers one, calls Monday neutral: roughly -1.3% to +0.6%. Ham Radio, which hears the chatter first, leans lower: about -1.5% to +0.4%.

7/ Both see more room to fall than to rise before the meeting, and both flag the same thing: whether the weekend brings Gulf headlines that send oil past $120.

8/ The bull case in one breath: oil eases, the 10-year holds under 5%, a patient-sounding Fed gives a tired market permission to breathe. The bear case: oil past $120, yields through 5%, a Fed that sounds like it isn't done.

9/ The part that bothers me: corporate borrowing costs are already at the 96th percentile of their history. In my experience, that is where trouble shows first.

10/ History doesn't settle it. In 2004 the Fed raised at every meeting, said it would, and markets mostly shrugged - nothing was a surprise. In late 2018 it raised into a wobbling market, and the index fell close to 20% by Christmas.

11/ So the one release that could change the most is the only one. My list: the press conference wording, the 10-year versus 5%, Brent versus $120, and credit spreads.
